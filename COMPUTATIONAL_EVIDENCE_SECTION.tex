\section{Computational evidence}\label{sec:computational-evidence}

The manuscript construction was independently exercised on 6,253 complete witness schedules: 3,653 quartic cases and 2,600 cubic cases, with zero implementation failures.  This exhaustive finite construction audit is supporting evidence and not a replacement for the analytic witness proofs.

The additional script \texttt{audit/computational\_evidence.py} exports finite lower/upper tables over $n$ and $P$.  For example, at $n=20$ the quartic and cubic witnesses give the following best lower bounds against the common constructive upper bound.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{c|rrrrr}
$P$ & 1 & 2 & 5 & 10 & 19 \\
\hline
best lower bound & 12 & 7 & 4 & 3 & 3 \\
upper bound & 21 & 12 & 6 & 4 & 3
\end{tabular}
\caption{Representative finite state bounds for $n=20$.  The stronger even-$n$ cubic witness is included in the best lower bound.}
\label{tab:dynamic-regular-finite}
\end{table}

The table makes two features visible: the finite gap shrinks as more periodic timing information is supplied, and the exact three-state threshold occurs at $P\ge n-1$.  The audit deliberately reports the interval rather than treating the near-exact theorem as an exact formula outside the regimes proved in the manuscript.
